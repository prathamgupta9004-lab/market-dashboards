Trend participation

How to read this

Share of stocks closing above their own 10, 20, 50 and 200-day moving averages. Above 70% on the 50-day is a broad, strong market; below 30% is washed out. Rising lines from low levels are what new uptrends usually look like.

How to read this

The line adds up (advancers − decliners) every day since March 2021; the bars show each day's net. A rising line means most stocks are participating. Index highs with a falling A/D line mean a narrow rally led by a few large stocks.

New 52-week highs minus lows

How to read this

Each bar is the number of stocks closing at a new 52-week high minus those at a new 52-week low (closing prices). Persistent positive bars confirm an uptrend; expanding new lows warn of broad weakness even if the index holds up.

Source: NSE daily bhavcopy, adjusted closes ·
Momentum thrust (±4% days)

How to read this

Number of stocks up at least 4% (mint) and down at least 4% (red) each day. Sudden bursts of 4% gainers after a decline (a "thrust") often mark the start of new rallies; clusters of 4% losers show forced selling.

Source: NSE daily bhavcopy ·

How to read this

How many stocks in the universe fell into each return bucket over the chosen period. A healthy market has its bulk on the right; a lopsided left side means selling is broad, not just in a few names.

The rules

Distribution day: Nifty closes down 0.2% or more on higher turnover than the previous session (turnover = traded value of its constituents). It expires after 25 sessions, or once the index closes 5% above that day's close.

Downgrade to Under Pressure: 5 or more live distribution days while in a confirmed uptrend.

Downgrade to Correction: 7+ distribution days, or the index falls 10% from its peak, or it is below its 50-day average and 7% off the peak with 4+ distribution days, or it undercuts the low that the last rally started from.

Rally Attempt: the first up day after a new correction low. It resets if the index makes a lower low.

Upgrade to Confirmed Uptrend (follow-through day): on day 4 or later of a rally attempt, the index gains 1.25%+ on higher turnover. Distribution days are cleared. From Under Pressure, the trend is re-confirmed once distribution days fall to 3 or fewer and the index is back above its 50-day average.

Sessions with turnover below half the 20-day median (special / Muhurat sessions) are skipped. These are mechanical O'Neil-style rules, the same ones the Stage 2 scanner uses.

FII / DII cash · provisional

How to read this

NSE's provisional end-of-day figures for the cash market (NSE + BSE + MSEI), ₹ Cr. NSE publishes only the latest day, so this history grows by one day each evening. Rolling sums show the recent direction; the streak counts consecutive net-buy or net-sell days.

Who bought · NSE category-wise turnover

How to read this

Net buying (buy − sell value, ₹ Cr) on NSE's cash market by client category. FPI figures are as reported by NSDL; "Others" covers corporates, trusts, NPS, DFIs, NBFCs and proprietary trades (NSE notes it can also include some FPI flows, so it may overlap the FPI bar). NSE publishes this with a lag of a day or more.

Market temperature

How to read this

For each broad index: the percentile of today's P/E and P/B within its own history since 2005 (or launch), and the inverse percentile of its dividend yield, averaged. The temperature averages Nifty 50, Nifty 500, Midcap 150, Smallcap 250 and Microcap 250. 0 = cheapest ever, 100 = dearest ever; 50 = typical.

Weekly trailing P/E (or P/B, dividend yield) published by NSE Indices. The dashed lines are the long-run median and the 20th / 80th percentiles of the index's own history. How to read this

Index P/E divided by Nifty 50 P/E. Above the long-run median = the smaller index trades at a richer premium than usual.

How to read this

Nifty 50 earnings yield (100 ÷ P/E) minus the India 10-year government bond yield, month-end. More negative than usual = equities offer less earnings per rupee relative to risk-free bonds than they typically have.
