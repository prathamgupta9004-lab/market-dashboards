How to read this

Each company's latest quarter is compared with the same quarter last year on the same basis (consolidated where filed). Margin is the EBITDA margin (PBT before exceptional items + finance cost + depreciation − other income, over revenue); for banks, NBFCs and insurers it is the net margin.

"X of Y" counts companies in this dashboard's universe (NSE, market cap ≥ ₹300 Cr) that filed the previous quarter or the same quarter last year. The minimum market-cap chips remove tiny bases whose growth rates swamp medians.

How to read this

Columns: this quarter, the previous quarter, the same quarter last year, then YoY and QoQ change (▲ ▼). Margin change is in basis points (100 bps = 1 percentage point).

Result day is the first session after the filing (filings after 3:30 pm count from the next day): its return, and versus the Nifty 500. Gap is that day's open against the previous close. 5d / 20d drift is the return from the result-day close over the next 5 / 20 sessions, versus the Nifty 500.

Surprise vs own trend is this quarter's YoY growth minus the average YoY growth of the previous four quarters. There is no analyst consensus here.

Warning flags

Quarter
How to read this
  • Other income: other income is 30% or more of profit before tax (skipped for banks, NBFCs and insurers, where it is core income).
  • Exceptional items: an exceptional gain is 25% or more of PBT, or profit rose only because of one (PBT before exceptional items fell YoY).
  • Receivables: trade receivables grew 20% or more in a year and at least 20 points faster than revenue (half-year revenue where filed). Needs the balance sheet, which companies file with September and March results.
  • Cash conversion: half-year cash from operations is under a quarter of half-year profit (or negative) (September filings carry the half-year cash-flow statement).
  • Margin collapse: EBITDA margin (net margin for financials) down 5 points or more YoY, or EBITDA turned negative.

A flag is a prompt to read the filing, not a verdict. The checks only run where the filing has the numbers; the counts show how many companies each check covered.
